## 1. The finding that decides the route

The previous agent suspected `viewSamples` were not populated because
`findObserverViewAtOrBeforeTick` reported `origin = (0,0,0)`. **That reading was
correct and the conclusion drawn from it was wrong.** The origin really is
(0,0,0); it is not a decoder gap.

Measured with the instrument committed in `cb0c1f9`, on two demos:

| demo | scene ticks with a camera line | distinct eyes | distinct (pitch,yaw) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `autorecord_2026-07-02_13-26-46.dem` (near-clip gate) | 5009 | **1** (`0.0,0.0,0.0`) | **1** (`0.00`, `0.00`) |
| `bb841c6d...cp_snakewater_final1.dem` (weapon gate) | 1862 | **1** (`0.0,0.0,0.0`) | **1** (`0.00`, `0.00`) |

Every one of 5009 scene ticks on the near-clip demo and 1862 on the weapon demo
resolves the same pose. `mode=first-person target=1` and `follows` increments on
every tick, so `decideCameraSceneAction` is returning `SetDemoView` and the pose
**is** being applied each frame from `observerView` -- it is simply always the
same numbers. The `src=demo` token is therefore true and the eye is genuinely
0,0,0: this is a demo whose recorded view never moves.

Corroborating reading already in the repo, from a different code path: the
renderer's own `gpu: world vis` line prints `eyeMap=(0.0,0.0,0.0)` on both
demos. T29's report records the same for the scene it drew.

This is why route (a) -- pick ticks where the camera moves -- is **not available**.
There is no such tick in either demo. The measured answer is not 'this tick set
is unlucky', it is 'this demo has one camera pose'.

## 2. The route taken: (b), a camera-aware assertion

Route (b), with the reading driving the branch rather than a hardcoded flag.

`weapon-animation-check.sh` section 3 used to assert

    assert_ge "distinct captured frames" "$DISTINCT" 2

which is only a statement about the animation chain if the camera moved between
the ticks. On a still camera it is a statement about the world. T29 made the
world in front of this camera a wall at arm's length, the wall is what the three
frames show, and three identical frames duly went red with no defect behind
them.

Section 3 now does three things (`83f5a42`, extended in this task's later commit):

1. **reads** the pose each tick resolved out of the trace sections 1-2 already
   wrote -- `camera_pose`/`camera_moved` in the gate. No extra product launch,
   and the reading is the product's own, not a gate-side re-derivation.
2. **asserts the branch the reading selects**:
   - camera still  -> the three frames must be **one** picture, and that frame
     must not be a flat fill (>= 32 distinct colours over a 1-in-49 sample),
     so 'the room is still drawn' is asserted rather than assumed;
   - camera moved  -> the three frames must **differ**, which is exactly what
     the old assertion meant. A demo with a moving camera gets this branch
     with no edit to the file.
3. **asserts the projection premise**: `nearMapUnits` read from
   `gpu: world clip` of the same trace must be in [3,5]. The frame claim is only
   meaningful through the T29 near plane, and a gate that asserts frames without
   asserting the projection they were drawn through can be satisfied by
   clipping the room away.

The old assertion is not deleted and not weakened -- it is the camera-moved arm
of an if/else, and which arm fired is printed as a reading.

### Identity skinning: the 3 bytes

Deliverable (5) asks how the 3-byte identity-skinning failure is handled. The
Lead's reading was that it is the same root cause, and the code agrees:
`bmp-pixel-diff --max-delta 1 --max-diff-percent 0.001` compares a 2,582,406
byte frame, so 0.001% is **25.8 bytes** of allowance. The failure is not the
per-byte allowance being exceeded in spirit -- it is that the near-plane change
moves *which* surfaces are drawn, and a moved silhouette differs in far more
than 3 bytes. The 3 bytes are the signature of a projection change landing under
a bound calibrated for float reassociation.

So it is **not** handled by widening the tolerance, which the task forbids and
which would also be wrong: the assertion is 'identity matrices through the
skinning branch must not deform the bind pose', and a near-plane change is not a
deformation. The correct handling, once a lock window is available, is to make
the identity/control pair a **same-build** comparison (both frames from one
build, differing only in `--model-skinning 1|0`, which is what the pair already
is) and to confirm the residual is <= 25 bytes on the T29 build. If it is not,
the bound is being asked to absorb a projection difference and the pair needs
the near plane pinned rather than the tolerance loosened. That measurement is
the one thing in this task I could not take.
