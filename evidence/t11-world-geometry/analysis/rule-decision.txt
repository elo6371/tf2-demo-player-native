T11 third round -- the winding decision, by first-hit oracle
===========================================================

The question this round answers
-------------------------------
Two rounds of winding work left a contradiction: the shipped rule (brush wound
along its plane normal, displacement against it) draws the first-person frame at
0.8953 of its no-cull maximum, and the top-down frame at 0.4683. Every one of the
eleven rules captured in round two traded one view for the other, so the
conclusion on file was "no plane-derived rule draws both views". That conclusion
was true and useless: it did not say which rule to ship, and it left
`misfaced=53724` as an unexplained open defect.

What changed this round is the instrument, not the philosophy.

1. The premise was wrong: the buffer ships one convention, not two
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Round two's class split was derived from the face POLYGON census. The buffer is
not the polygon: the brush fan emits `pushTriangle(polygon[0], polygon[i + 1],
polygon[i])`, the reverse of the polygon's own order. Measured on the emitted
order, before any rule (`geometry pre-rule`):

  class=brush         alongPlane=41624  againstPlane=396    (99.1% one way)
  class=displacement  alongPlane=46856  againstPlane=142    (99.7% one way)

Both classes ship the SAME convention. There was never a class split to
reproduce, and the rule that reproduced one was flipping 38614 displacement
triangles that did not need flipping.

2. The oracle: the first surface along a ray must be the surface that is drawn
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A back-face cull is correct exactly when, for every ray from the camera, the
FIRST triangle that ray meets survives the cull. That is a statement about the
data, not about any rule, and it needs no shading, no materials and no
thresholds:

  - from above (`--ray-down N`): a vertical ray per grid cell across the map
    bounds; keep the topmost triangle at that (x, y); it must be drawn by a
    camera looking down, i.e. its emitted winding must face up.
  - from the eye (`--ray-eye x,y,z N`): a sphere of directions; keep the first
    triangle each ray meets; it must be drawn, i.e. its emitted winding must face
    the eye.

Every triangle carries `windingFlipped`, so the census can recover the STORED
winding and therefore score any rule without rebuilding it. Both oracles reduce
to four cells -- (class, side) -- and a rule is a keep/flip choice per cell.

4. What the table says
----------------------
a. The shipped rule (mask 03) is the eye's optimum (0.820) and the top-down
   view's near-worst (0.311). It is not a compromise; it is a choice of one
   view.
b. The stored order (mask 15) is the best max-min of the sixteen (0.711 /
   0.729) and needs no rule at all -- the emission simply stops rewriting data
   it cannot justify. It costs the eye nine points against the shipped rule.
c. Mask 07 -- brush keeps the map's order, displacement is oriented to its
   face's side-aware outward direction -- is within 0.003 of the max-min optimum
   and keeps the eye within five points of its own optimum. It is the shipped
   rule's smallest functional delta that recovers the top-down view.
d. The two views disagree in exactly TWO cells: brush/side-1 and
   displacement/side-0. Every point of the eye's score above 0.771 is bought by
   flipping displacement/side-0, and every point of the top-down view above 0.708
   is bought by flipping brush/side-1. No rule in this family escapes that
   trade-off, and the two cells are the residual, named.

5. Why the previous round's coverage numbers pointed the wrong way
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Flat-colour coverage counts a pixel as "world present" whether the surface drawn
there is the near one or a far one seen through a culled near surface. A rule
that culls the nearest surface therefore still scores: the frame fills with the
surfaces behind it. The no-cull reference (0.9228) is the silhouette, not a
correctness ceiling, and the shipped rule reached 0.8953 of it while drawing the
wrong side for 18% of the eye's rays. Coverage was the wrong metric; the
first-hit oracle is the right one, and it is why the shipped rule's 0.8953 and
its 0.311 sit in the same sentence without contradicting each other.

6. What ships
-------------
Mask 07, in `native/src/bsp_map.cpp` (one condition, no class sign, no side
folding): brush triangles keep the order the map stores, displacement triangles
are reversed when they point away from their face's side-aware outward
direction. Readings move as the table predicts:

  flippedTriangles   47252 -> 8514
  misfaced           53724 -> 15126
  displacedMisfaced  38614 -> 0
  top-down first hits drawn   0.311 -> 0.708
  eye first hits drawn        0.820 -> 0.771

Reproduce: `world_geometry_probe --tf-root <tf> --map maps/pl_odyssey.bsp
--eye -2423.6,5566.0,-1280.0 --ray-down 96 --ray-eye -2423.6,5566.0,-1280.0 24`
